Giant Gourami

Osphronemus goramy

The Giant Gourami is a large freshwater fish introduced to Sri Lanka and is one of the biggest species in the gourami family. It is found in ponds, reservoirs, lakes, marshes, canals, and slow-moving rivers with abundant aquatic vegetation. This hardy fish can tolerate a wide range of water conditions and, like other gouramies, has a labyrinth organ that allows it to breathe atmospheric air and survive in low-oxygen environments. The Giant Gourami is a slow-moving but powerful fish with a broad body and strong jaws. It is an omnivorous species that feeds on aquatic plants, fruits, algae, insects, small crustaceans, and other organic matter. Adults are generally calm but can become territorial, especially when breeding. Males build bubble nests and guard the eggs and young until they are able to survive independently. In Sri Lanka, the Giant Gourami is mainly kept as an ornamental and food fish due to its large size, attractive appearance, and ability to adapt to different freshwater habitats. However, as an introduced species, it may affect native aquatic ecosystems by competing with local fish for food and space.
